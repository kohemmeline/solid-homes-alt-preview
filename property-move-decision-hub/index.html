The Property Move Decision Hub: Sell First or Buy First?

Moving between homes in Singapore is one decision hiding behind five smaller ones — sale proceeds, CPF refund, next-home budget, loan capacity and timing. This hub walks you through all of them as one system, with a calculator that turns your numbers into a clear next-step answer.

Updated 15 August 2026 · For sellers, upgraders, right-sizers and landlords

01

The one question behind every property move

Direct Answer

Your next home’s budget is not what you can borrow. It is what is left after you sell your current home, pay off your housing loan, refund the CPF you owe, cover transaction costs — and protect your retirement buffer. The maximum a bank will lend is a ceiling, not a target.

Most people ask “can I afford the next condo?” and get an answer about loans. The smarter question is “what can I safely do with my current home, CPF and cash without putting my retirement at risk?” That is the question this hub answers.

02

The 6-step move decision framework

1

Estimate your sale price

Start with a realistic resale value of your current home — not the optimistic list price. Use comparable recent transactions in your area.

2

Deduct the outstanding housing loan

Whatever is left after your principal is paid off is your gross sale proceeds. For a bank-loan HDB, confirm the loan settlement figure.

3

Account for the CPF refund

When you sell, CPF savings used for the property — including accrued interest — generally must be refunded to your CPF accounts. This reduces your cash proceeds but rebuilds your retirement balance.

4

Subtract transaction costs

Property agent commission, legal fees, stamp duties and any applicable seller’s duties eat into proceeds. Plan for them explicitly.

5

Decide your loan and CPF strategy

How much debt can you service comfortably? How much CPF do you want to redeploy versus keep — or refund — for retirement adequacy? A bank loan and an HDB loan carry different cash-downpayment rules.

6

Sequence the move

Do you sell first, buy first, or overlap? The choice changes your cash flow, your temporary-housing situation and your exposure to market timing.

03

Calculate your next-home budget

Plug in your numbers to see how a sale converts into a next-home budget. This is a planning approximation — exact figures depend on your loan settlement, CPF statement and transaction costs.

04

Sell first or buy first?

Selling first

You know your exact sale proceeds before you commit to buying, which removes the biggest uncertainty and eases cash-flow pressure. The trade-off: you may need temporary accommodation between homes, and the replacement home you want could move or be taken off the market while you wait.

Buying first

You secure the replacement home on your own timeline and avoid a gap in housing. The trade-off: you can end up carrying financing and timing risk while your current home is still unsold, and you may face a narrower window to complete both transactions.

The right sequence depends on your eligibility (including ABSD exposure where it applies), CPF timing, loan capacity, and how quickly your current home can realistically sell. There is no universally correct answer — only the answer that fits your numbers and your risk tolerance.

Frequently asked questions

How much property can I afford after selling my current home?

Start with your expected sale price, deduct the outstanding housing loan, the required CPF refund and transaction costs, then combine the remaining cash and CPF with the loan amount you can prudently service. The maximum a bank will lend is not automatically the amount you should spend — your retirement needs and monthly buffer should also be modelled.

Should I sell before buying my next home?

Selling first gives clearer sale proceeds and reduces cash-flow pressure, but you may need temporary accommodation. Buying first secures the replacement home earlier but can leave you carrying more financing and timing risk. The right sequence depends on eligibility, ABSD exposure where applicable, CPF timing, loan capacity and how quickly your current home can realistically sell.

Can I use all my CPF OA for my next property?

CPF Ordinary Account savings can generally be used for eligible housing expenses, but the amount available and usable depends on the property, the loan and the applicable CPF housing rules. A sound purchase plan should also consider retirement adequacy rather than treating the maximum CPF amount as the ideal amount to spend.

Why does a bank-loan HDB purchase need cash?

CPF states that for an HDB flat financed with a bank loan, buyers must first pay the required cash components — including the minimum cash downpayment and applicable option fees — before CPF Ordinary Account savings can be used for the remaining eligible purchase amounts.

Can I buy an HDB before selling my condo?

The removal of the 15-month wait-out for eligible non-subsidised HDB resale purchases changes the timing options for some private-property owners, but your HFE, financing, disposal requirements and the specific housing route still need to be checked before committing to an OTP.
